# Understand the safety labels

deck: ch0-01
chapter: Front matter
tier: intro
summary: Read the SXC-1's Warning, Caution, and prohibition symbols correctly.
cite: guide-book 3 "use the product correctly"

Before the first sound, learn what the SXC-1's Warning, Caution, and prohibition
symbols mean so you can recognize an unsafe action immediately.

## What the Warning label means

type: quiz
id: q-0-01
cite: guide-book 3 "Indicates content that could result in death or serious injury."
tags: front-matter, intro

At the head of the safety material, items are labeled either Warning or Caution.
What does the **Warning** label indicate?

- [x] Content that could result in death or serious injury
- [ ] Content that could result in minor injury or property damage
- [ ] Something you must not do
- [ ] Something you must do

### Why

Warning is the severe label: ignoring a Warning item risks death or serious injury.
The lighter outcomes — minor injury or property damage — belong to the Caution label,
and the "must not do" / "must do" meanings belong to the graphic symbols, not the
labels.

## What the Caution label means

type: quiz
id: q-0-02
cite: guide-book 3 "Indicates content that could result in minor injury or property damage."
tags: front-matter, intro

The safety material's other label is **Caution**. What kind of content does it
indicate?

### Answer

distractor: Content that could result in death or serious injury.

Content that could result in minor injury or property damage — less severe than
Warning, which covers death or serious injury.

### Why

Knowing the two labels apart tells you how seriously to treat each precaution at a
glance: a Caution item protects you and your gear from lesser harm, while a Warning
item is potentially life-threatening.

## The two graphic symbols

type: quiz
id: q-0-03
cite: guide-book 3 "the example at left means"
tags: front-matter, intro

Besides the Warning/Caution labels, the safety items carry two graphic symbols: a
crossed-out circle and a filled circle. State what each symbol marks.

### Answer

distractor: The crossed-out circle marks Required; the filled circle marks Prohibited.

The crossed-out circle marks "something you must not do" (Prohibited). The filled
circle marks "something you must do" (Required).

### Why

The labels grade severity; the symbols grade direction. A Prohibited item is an action
to avoid, a Required item is an action you must actually take — reading the symbol
tells you which kind of instruction you are looking at before you read a word.

# Last cautions, first habits

deck: ch0-04
chapter: Front matter
tier: core
summary: Learn three everyday habits for volume, the microphone, and the display.
cite: guide-book 7 "Operating precautions"

These three operating habits protect your ears and help the SXC-1 behave as expected
in every session.

## Why MAIN VOL goes to minimum first

type: quiz
id: q-0-31
cite: guide-book 7 "The MAIN VOL of this unit is shared between the headphones and the speaker."
tags: front-matter, core

Before turning the power on or connecting headphones, you should set MAIN VOL to
minimum and then raise it gradually. Why?

- [x] MAIN VOL is shared between the headphones and the speaker, and the actual volume differs between them at the same slider position
- [ ] The power switch does not work unless MAIN VOL is at minimum
- [ ] MAIN VOL also controls the input level for recording
- [ ] The speaker is muted whenever headphones are connected, so the slider has no effect

### Why

Because one slider serves two outputs whose loudness differs at the same position, a
level that was comfortable on the speaker can be painfully loud the moment headphones
go in. Starting from minimum and raising gradually protects your hearing — be
especially careful when switching from the speaker to headphones.

## Using the built-in microphone well

type: quiz
id: q-0-32
cite: guide-book 7 "designed for close-range sound pickup"
tags: front-matter, core

The built-in microphone has a design constraint that dictates how you position
whatever you are recording — and one recommended habit before any important
recording. Name both.

### Answer

distractor: Keep the source far from the microphone and skip the test recording.

It is designed for close-range sound pickup, so keep whatever you are recording close
to the mic. Before an important recording, make a test recording first to check the
distance to the mic and the surrounding noise.

### Why

A close-range mic simply cannot capture distant material well — no setting fixes
that. The test recording catches distance and noise problems while they are still
cheap to fix.

## The display went dark after half an hour

type: quiz
id: q-0-33
cite: guide-book 7 "If no operation is performed for about 30 minutes, the display turns off automatically."
tags: front-matter, core

You leave this unit powered on and untouched. After about 30 minutes the display goes
dark, but a single button or pad operation brings it right back. Which feature did
you just see?

- [x] The screen saver function
- [ ] The Auto Power Off function
- [ ] A depleted battery shutting the power off
- [ ] The display brightness setting stepping down

### Why

The screen saver exists to prevent display burn-in: after about 30 minutes without
operation it turns only the display off, and any button or pad operation restores it.
The manual stresses that this is different from the Auto Power Off function, which
turns the whole unit's power off.

# Operating environment, quirks, and power behavior

deck: ch0-05
chapter: Front matter
tier: core
summary: Learn how USB power takes priority and how LINE OUT behaves.
cite: guide-book 7 "Operating environment"

Two practical power-and-output facts prevent surprises when batteries, USB power,
and external speakers are connected.

## Batteries in, adaptor connected: which wins?

type: quiz
id: q-0-34
cite: guide-book 8 "USB power takes priority"
tags: front-matter, core

Four batteries are installed, and a USB AC adaptor is also connected to the
POWER/DATA port. Which power supply does this unit run on?

- [x] USB power — it takes priority over the batteries
- [ ] The batteries — installed batteries always take priority
- [ ] Whichever was connected first
- [ ] Neither; this unit refuses to power on until one is removed

### Why

When a USB AC adaptor is connected to the POWER/DATA port while batteries are
installed, USB power takes priority. That is why leaving batteries in as a fallback
is safe — but also why a loose USB cable can still cut the power mid-recording, so
keep the cable securely connected.

## LINE OUT is connected — is the speaker quiet?

type: quiz
id: q-0-35
cite: guide-book 8 "sound is still output from this unit's speaker"
tags: front-matter, core

You connect an external speaker to the LINE OUT jack. What happens to this unit's own
built-in speaker, and how do you silence it if you want only the external speaker?

### Answer

distractor: Connecting LINE OUT automatically mutes the built-in speaker.

Sound is still output from this unit's speaker — connecting LINE OUT does not mute
it. To silence this unit's speaker, set MAIN VOL to minimum.

### Why

LINE OUT and the built-in speaker are independent outputs, so the mix you hear in the
room is both at once unless you pull MAIN VOL down. (Headphones in the PHONE jack are
the opposite case — they do take over from the speaker.)

## The note number for pad 1, Bank A

type: quiz
id: q-5-91
cite: midi 4 "Note mapping"
tags: midi, stretch

## Initialize the unit (optional, destructive)

type: drill
id: d-5-14
cite: guide-book 55 "Initializes the unit to its factory default state"
tags: leveling-up, stretch

Return this unit to its factory default state -- after protecting your work.
**This drill is optional and destructive**: if you initialize, the data you have
sampled will be deleted. Skip it, or do the backup step and stop there, if you
want to keep working with your current banks.

### Step

cite: guide-book 60 "make backups, and configure various settings"
check: The app reports a completed backup of the unit's data.

In the CASIO Sampler App, tap the gear icon at the top right and make a backup
of the unit (the same screen offers Restore for afterwards).

### Step

cite: guide-book 55 "After pressing the right directional button, confirm with the `A` button"
check: The display is asking you to confirm the initialization with the `A` button.

On the unit, long-press `EDIT` to open the system settings, select `Initialize`,
and press the right directional button.

### Step

cite: guide-book 55 "After initialization, the unit restarts."
check: The unit restarts on its own and comes back in its factory default state, sampled data gone.

Confirm with the `A` button.

### Why

Initialize is the only way back to a known-clean state, and the app's Backup /
Restore pair is what makes it survivable -- the sampled data it deletes cannot
be recovered any other way.
