Your phrase is chopped across four pads. This deck is about what comes next:
trimming the slices, playing them like an instrument, and splitting by hand when
the automatic beat split is not what you want.

## When auto chop recording stops

type: quiz
id: q-5-39
cite: guide-book 53 "When analysis of 4 beats × 4 = 4 measures is complete, recording ends automatically."
tags: leveling-up, core

You set auto chop to 4 beats on each of 4 pads. When does recording start, and when
does it end?

### Answer

Recording starts when the phrase is input, and ends automatically when analysis of
4 beats × 4 = 4 measures is complete.

## Trimming a distributed slice

type: quiz
id: q-5-40
cite: guide-book 53 "The start and end points of the distributed samples can be changed with waveform editing in SOUND EDIT"
tags: leveling-up, core

Auto chop cut a slice slightly wrong. Where do you fix its boundaries?

- [x] With waveform editing in `SOUND EDIT`
- [ ] Only by re-recording the whole phrase
- [ ] In the `GROUP` item
- [ ] In the system settings

### Why

Chopped slices are ordinary samples -- the same start-point and end-point editing
you used on single sounds works on each slice.

## Extending one slice

type: quiz
id: q-5-41
cite: guide-book 53 "You can also extend each sample's range forward or backward."
tags: leveling-up, stretch

Can you extend one distributed slice beyond the boundary auto chop gave it? What
happens to its neighbors?

### Answer

Yes -- each sample's range can be extended forward or backward, and doing so does
not affect the other distributed samples.

## The cost of extending a slice

type: quiz
id: q-5-42
cite: guide-book 53 "the sample may no longer connect smoothly with the samples before and after it"
tags: leveling-up, stretch

What is the warned-about downside of extending a distributed sample's range?

### Answer

The sample may no longer connect smoothly with the samples before and after it
when you play the slices in sequence.

## Before you edit: protect the original

type: quiz
id: q-5-43
cite: guide-book 53 "cannot be returned to its pre-edit state"
tags: leveling-up, core

Why does the manual recommend copying a sample by resampling *before* editing it?

- [x] An edited sample cannot be returned to its pre-edit state
- [ ] Editing is only allowed on copied samples
- [ ] Resampling improves the sound quality first
- [ ] The copy is needed to keep Beat Sync working

### Why

There is no undo for sample edits -- the resampled copy is your only way back to
the original sound.

## What "chopping" means

type: quiz
id: q-5-44
cite: guide-book 53 "a technique unique to samplers"
tags: leveling-up, intro

In sampler terms, what is "chopping"?

### Answer

Rearranging broken-up samples, or shifting their playback timing, to create new
phrases -- a technique unique to samplers.

## Chop plus grouping

type: quiz
id: q-5-45
cite: guide-book 53 "you can perform as if you were rearranging the song itself"
tags: leveling-up, stretch

What does setting your auto-chopped samples to the same group unlock when you tap
the pads freely?

### Answer

Each new tap cuts off the previous slice, so you can perform as if you were
rearranging the song itself.

## Perform a chop with grouped pads

type: drill
id: d-5-12
cite: guide-book 53 "Set auto-chopped samples to the same group"
tags: leveling-up, stretch

Turn your four chopped slices into a playable instrument by grouping them, then
rearrange the phrase live.

### Step

cite: guide-book 53 "Set auto-chopped samples to the same group"
check: Tapping any two slice pads in turn, the second tap cuts the first slice off cleanly.

In `SOUND EDIT`, set all four chopped pads' `GROUP` to the same number, then
return to Performance mode.

### Step

cite: guide-book 53 "rearranging broken-up samples or shifting their playback timing to create new phrases"
check: Your taps produce a coherent new phrase -- slices in a new order, each one silencing the last.

Tap the slice pads freely, out of order and on your own timing, until the
rearranged phrase sounds intentional. If a slice boundary bothers you, trim it
with waveform editing -- after resampling a safety copy first, since edits cannot
be undone.

## Splitting at your own timing

type: quiz
id: q-5-46
cite: guide-book 54 "you can also split at whatever timing you like"
tags: leveling-up, stretch

Instead of the automatic beat-by-beat split, how do you split a sample at your own
chosen points?

### Answer

In Sampling mode select `AUTO TRIGGER` or `MANUAL TRIGGER`, press one empty pad,
input the phrase, and press the next empty pad at each point where you want a
split.

## When manual-split recording starts

type: quiz
id: q-5-47
cite: guide-book 54 "recording starts at this point"
tags: leveling-up, stretch

In the split-at-your-own-timing procedure, when does recording start with
`MANUAL TRIGGER`, and when with `AUTO TRIGGER`?

### Answer

With `MANUAL TRIGGER`, recording starts the moment you press the first empty pad;
with `AUTO TRIGGER`, it starts when the phrase is input.

## Ending a manual split

type: quiz
id: q-5-48
cite: guide-book 54 "press the last pad again or end recording with the"
tags: leveling-up, stretch

You have pressed empty pads at each split point and the phrase is over. How do you
end the recording?

- [x] Press the last pad again, or end recording with the `REC` button
- [ ] Wait -- it always stops by itself after four measures
- [ ] Press the `▶/■` button
- [ ] Press the `EDIT` button

### Why

Only you know when the phrase is finished, so the manual split needs an explicit
stop -- either of the two actions works.
