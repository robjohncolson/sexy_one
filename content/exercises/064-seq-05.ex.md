# Step input and quantize

deck: seq-05
chapter: Part: Sequencer
tier: core

summary: Fix notes cell by cell with step input, and understand the 16th-note quantize grid.

A recorded pattern is never final. Here you edit it cell by cell -- erase with `B`,
enter with `A` -- and learn why the grid forgives small timing mistakes.

## When editing is possible

type: quiz
id: q-4-30
cite: guide-book 40 "Editing is also possible during recording/playback"
tags: sequencer, core

When can you edit a sequence's cells?

- [x] Both with recording/playback stopped and during recording/playback
- [ ] Only while recording
- [ ] Only while stopped
- [ ] Only while playback is running

### Why

Editing while the loop plays lets you hear each fix in context immediately; editing
while stopped gives you precision without the clock running. The unit allows both.

## Erasing and entering sounds at the cursor

type: quiz
id: q-4-31
cite: guide-book 40 "the sound the cursor is on is erased"
tags: sequencer, core

The cursor sits on a cell in the step grid. What do the `B` and `A` buttons do?

- [x] `B` erases the sound the cursor is on; `A` enters a sound at that timing
- [ ] `A` erases; `B` enters
- [ ] `B` moves the cursor; `A` plays the cell
- [ ] Both erase, at different volumes

### Why

It is the same OK/cancel pairing as everywhere else on the unit: `A` adds
(confirms), `B` takes away (cancels) -- here applied to a single cell of the grid.

## The smallest unit of sound

type: quiz
id: q-4-32
cite: guide-book 40 "one measure of 4/4 time divided into 16"
tags: sequencer, core

What is the smallest unit of sound handled in the SXC-1's Sequence mode?

- [x] The 16th note -- one measure of 4/4 time divided into 16
- [ ] The 8th note -- one measure divided into 8
- [ ] The 32nd note -- one measure divided into 32
- [ ] One full beat

### Why

This is why the pad input area draws exactly 16 cells per measure: one cell per 16th
note. Nothing can land between cells.

## What "quantize" means

type: quiz
id: q-4-33
cite: guide-book 40 "it is corrected to the nearest timing"
tags: sequencer, core

Your tap lands slightly off the grid -- less than a 16th note away. What does the
sequencer do, and what is this feature called?

### Answer

The timing is corrected to the nearest 16th-note timing. This kind of feature is
called "quantize".

## Silencing the metronome

type: quiz
id: q-4-34
cite: guide-book 41 "you can also turn it OFF with `METRONOME` in the EDIT function"
tags: sequencer, intro

You do not need the metronome sound while working on patterns. What can you do?

- [x] Turn it OFF with `METRONOME` in the EDIT function
- [ ] Turn the `MAIN VOL` slider down
- [ ] Long-press the `B` button
- [ ] It cannot be silenced during recording

### Why

The metronome has its own switch, so you can silence the click without touching the
volume of your pattern itself.

## Naming the first input style

type: quiz
id: q-4-35
cite: guide-book 41 "Recording your pad performance exactly as you play it is called"
tags: sequencer, core

What is it called when the sequencer records your pad performance exactly as you
play it?

- [x] Real-time input
- [ ] Step input
- [ ] Quantize
- [ ] Overdubbing

### Why

Real-time input is the "perform it" style -- perfect for intuitive pattern making,
with quantize quietly tidying your timing behind the scenes.

## When playing everything at once is too hard

type: quiz
id: q-4-36
cite: guide-book 41 "it is also OK to input one pad at a time"
tags: sequencer, core

A drum pattern needs several pads at once and your fingers cannot keep up. What does
the manual suggest?

### Answer

Input one pad at a time: first perform just the hi-hat, then perform the kick while
listening to it, and finally layer the snare on top.

## Naming the second input style

type: quiz
id: q-4-37
cite: guide-book 41 "inputting by filling in the on-screen cells one note at a time"
tags: sequencer, core

What is the method of inputting by filling in the on-screen cells one note at a time
called?

- [x] Step input
- [ ] Real-time input
- [ ] Cell mode
- [ ] Pattern entry

### Why

Step input is the "draw it" style: slower than performing, but exact -- and using
both styles where each shines is one of the joys of the sequencer.
