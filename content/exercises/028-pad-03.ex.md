## What Beat Sync does

type: quiz
id: q-2-19
cite: guide-book 18 "This feature makes multiple looped sounds play so they sound natural when layered on top of each other."
tags: beat-sync, core

What does turning Beat Sync ON actually do for you when you layer multiple looped
sounds?

- [x] It makes the looped sounds play together so the layering sounds natural
- [ ] It changes the FX1 and FX2 effect types automatically
- [ ] It raises MAIN VOL to a preset level
- [ ] It records a new sample onto an empty pad

### Why

Without Beat Sync, two looped sounds with different tempos would drift out of time with
each other; Beat Sync's time stretch and quantize keep them locked together.

## Beat Sync's two components

type: quiz
id: q-2-21
cite: guide-book 18 "Time stretch"
tags: beat-sync, core

Beat Sync is really two features working together. Which two?

- [x] Time stretch and quantize
- [ ] Time stretch and pitch shift
- [ ] Quantize and MASTER BPM reset
- [ ] Looping and grouping

### Why

Time stretch matches a second loop's tempo to the one already playing; quantize
corrects the timing of your tap. Together they make layered loops land in time and in
tempo.

## What time stretch does to a second sound

type: quiz
id: q-2-22
cite: guide-book 18 "matched to the tempo of the sound that is already playing"
tags: beat-sync, core

A sound with a BPM setting is playing. You start another sound whose BPM is different.
With Beat Sync ON, what happens to the second sound — and what happens to its pitch?

### Answer

The second sound is played back matched to the tempo of the sound that is already
playing. For a sound that has a pitch, only the tempo changes — the pitch stays the
same.

### Why

That is the point of time stretch: loops recorded at different tempos still lock
together, without the chipmunk effect that speeding a sound up would normally cause.

## The BPM at the upper right of the display

type: quiz
id: q-2-23
cite: guide-book 18 "The BPM at the upper right is the sound's tempo information"
tags: beat-sync, core

When you tap a pad that has a BPM setting, a BPM value appears at the upper right of
the display. What is it, and where else does the same value appear?

- [x] The sound's tempo information; the same value shows in the MASTER BPM area
- [ ] The bank number; the same value shows after `PAD:`
- [ ] The effect strength; the same value shows next to `FX1:`
- [ ] A countdown until the loop stops; it appears nowhere else

### Why

Reading BPM and MASTER BPM together is how you see time stretch working: the first
tagged sound you play sets MASTER BPM, and everything after follows it.

## Which tempo later sounds follow

type: quiz
id: q-2-24
cite: guide-book 18 "it is played at the MASTER BPM tempo, regardless of its original tempo"
tags: beat-sync, core

A MASTER BPM is set and you play another sound. At what tempo does that sound play?

- [x] At the MASTER BPM tempo, regardless of its original tempo
- [ ] At its own original tempo
- [ ] At the average of the two tempos
- [ ] At MASTER BPM only if its own BPM is higher

### Why

MASTER BPM is the session's single clock: while it is set, every sound you start is
pulled to it, which is what keeps a stack of loops feeling like one band.

## What resets the MASTER BPM

type: quiz
id: q-2-25
cite: guide-book 18 "Stopping playback resets the MASTER BPM"
tags: beat-sync, core

What resets the MASTER BPM?

- [x] Stopping playback
- [ ] Tapping any one-shot pad
- [ ] Turning the FX1 dial
- [ ] Switching banks with the `A`-`D` buttons

### Why

Once playback stops, the clock is cleared: the next BPM-tagged sound you play sets a
fresh MASTER BPM and plays at its own tempo.

## What quantize corrects

type: quiz
id: q-2-26
cite: guide-book 18 "automatically corrects slight timing errors in when you tap the pad"
tags: beat-sync, core

With Beat Sync ON, you trigger a second looped sound slightly off the beat. What does
the quantize component do?

- [x] It corrects the slight timing error, aligning the loop starts precisely
- [ ] It mutes the second loop until the next bar
- [ ] It slows the first loop down to meet your tap
- [ ] It shortens the second loop to fit

### Why

Quantize forgives imprecise taps: the loops start exactly together even when your
finger was a little early or late — which is what makes live layering feel tight.
