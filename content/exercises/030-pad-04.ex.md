## What appears when you tap a pad

type: quiz
id: q-2-27
cite: guide-book 19 "a vertical line moves from left to right across the waveform"
tags: waveform, intro

When you tap a pad, the display shows the sound's waveform. What moves across it while
the sound plays?

- [x] A vertical line, from left to right
- [ ] The MASTER BPM value, counting down
- [ ] The bank indicator, cycling `A` to `D`
- [ ] The pad number, blinking

### Why

The moving line is a playhead: it shows how far through the sound playback has
gotten, so you can time your next tap to the start of the loop.

## Reading the waveform's axes

type: quiz
id: q-2-28
cite: guide-book 19 "Vertical axis: loudness of the sound"
tags: waveform, intro

On the waveform display, what do the vertical axis and the horizontal axis each show?

- [x] Vertical = loudness of the sound; horizontal = passage of time
- [ ] Vertical = pitch; horizontal = loudness
- [ ] Vertical = tempo; horizontal = pitch
- [ ] Vertical = effect strength; horizontal = bank number

### Why

Reading loudness-over-time at a glance tells you where a sound's hits fall — which
is exactly what you need when timing a tap to the start of a rhythm.

## Finger drum along with a looped sound

type: drill
id: d-2-03
cite: guide-book 19 "Listen to the rhythm you will use as a reference"
tags: pad-play, core

Build the book's core layering exercise in BANK 1: reference rhythm, rhythm without
drums, your own drums, then bass and chords on top.

### Step

cite: guide-book 19 "Tap pad `13` and listen to the drums + percussion rhythm."
check: The drums and percussion loop plays steadily — this is your reference rhythm.
verify: pad 13 bank A

Tap pad `13` and listen to the drums + percussion rhythm you will use as a reference.

### Step

cite: guide-book 19 "tap pad `15` and the rhythm changes to one without the drums"
check: The rhythm continues, but the drums drop out of it.
verify: pad 15 bank A

Tap pad `15` — the rhythm changes to one without the drums, leaving space for yours.

### Step

cite: guide-book 19 "tap pads `1` and `2` to layer drums on top"
check: Your kick and snare taps sound on top of the percussion loop, and the waveform's vertical line keeps moving left to right.
verify: pad 1 bank A

While picturing the rhythm you listened to a moment ago, tap pads `1` and `2` to layer
your own drums on top.

### Step

cite: guide-book 20 "time your tap to the start of the rhythm and tap pad `9`"
check: The bass phrase loops together with the percussion — you now have a percussion + bass loop.
verify: pad 9 bank A

With the percussion loop still playing, time your tap to the start of the rhythm and
tap pad `9` to layer in the bass.

### Step

cite: guide-book 20 "try tapping pads `5` through `8` in order to layer keyboard sounds on top"
check: Each tap of pads `5`-`8` plays a different chord over the rhythm + bass loop, like a keyboardist switching chords.
verify: pad 5 bank A

While the rhythm + bass loop plays, tap pads `5` through `8` in order to layer keyboard
sounds on top.

## Stop multiple loops at once

type: drill
id: d-2-04
cite: guide-book 20 "you can also stop the sound with the `▶/■` button"
tags: pad-play, intro

Start two looped sounds, then stop them both with a single button press.

### Step

cite: guide-book 20 "Play the rhythm + bass loop (pads 13 and 9)"
check: Both loops play at the same time — percussion plus bass.
verify: pad 13 bank A

In BANK 1, tap pad `13` and then pad `9` so two loops are running together.

### Step

cite: guide-book 20 "Use this method to stop multiple loops at once."
check: Every loop stops at the same moment, and the sounding pads return to their original colors.
verify: cc 102 127

Press the `▶/■` button once. Besides tapping each sounding pad again, this is the way
to stop all loops at once.
