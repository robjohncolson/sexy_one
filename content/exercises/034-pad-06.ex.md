# Grouping, bank switching, and the effect dials

deck: pad-06
chapter: Part: Pad play
tier: core
requires: pad-03
summary: Predict when a second pad switches or layers the sound, switch banks safely, and meet the FX1/FX2 effect lines.

Whether a second tap replaces or joins the sound playing is decided by pad grouping.
Learn the rule, hear it, avoid the bank-switching trap, and start shaping the sound
with `FX1` and `FX2`.

## What decides switch versus layer

type: quiz
id: q-2-38
cite: guide-book 22 "This is determined by pad grouping."
tags: grouping, core

A sound is playing and you tap another pad. What determines whether the new sound
replaces the playing one or is layered on top of it?

- [x] Pad grouping
- [ ] The order the pads were tapped in the current session
- [ ] Whether the `LOOP` button is lit
- [ ] The MASTER BPM value

### Why

Grouping is the routing rule of pad play: pads that share a group replace each other,
everything else stacks — so you can swap rhythms while a bassline keeps running.

## What happens between grouped pads

type: quiz
id: q-2-39
cite: guide-book 22 "The sound switches to the pad tapped later"
tags: grouping, core

Rhythm pads `13` and `14` are in the same group. You tap `13`, then tap `14`. What do
you hear?

- [x] The sound switches to the `14` rhythm
- [ ] Both rhythms play layered together
- [ ] Pad `14` waits until pad `13` finishes
- [ ] Both sounds stop

### Why

Grouped pads are exclusive: the later tap wins. That is exactly what you want for
rhythms — two drum loops at once would fight each other.

## What happens across groups

type: quiz
id: q-2-40
cite: guide-book 22 "The sound of the pad tapped later is layered on top"
tags: grouping, core

Bass pad `9` is playing and you tap piano pad `11` — pads in different groups. What do
you hear?

- [x] The bass `9` sound and the piano `11` sound layered together
- [ ] The piano replaces the bass
- [ ] The bass replaces the piano
- [ ] Neither sound, until you press the play/stop button

### Why

Ungrouped or differently grouped pads stack — that is how one player builds bass,
piano, and rhythm into a full arrangement in real time.

In BANK1, which pads belong to the rhythm, bass, and piano groups?

- [x] Rhythm = pads 13-16, bass = pads 9-10, piano = pads 11-12
- [ ] Rhythm = pads 1-4, bass = pads 5-8, piano = pads 9-12
- [ ] Rhythm = pads 13-16, bass = pads 11-12, piano = pads 9-10
- [ ] All 16 pads are one group

### Why

Knowing the group boundaries tells you in advance which taps will swap and which will
stack: rhythms swap among 13-16, bass swaps within 9-10, piano within 11-12, and
everything else layers.

## The bank-switching trap during playback

type: quiz
id: q-2-43
cite: guide-book 23 "Switching banks with the directional buttons during playback interrupts the sound."
tags: banks, core

You plan to layer sounds from two banks during a performance. Why should the banks you
need already be on the `A`-`D` buttons before you start?

- [x] Switching banks with the directional buttons during playback interrupts the sound
- [ ] The directional buttons stop working during playback
- [ ] The `A`-`D` buttons are the only buttons that light up
- [ ] Loops play at the wrong tempo unless a bank button is lit

### Why

The `A`-`D` buttons switch banks seamlessly; the directional buttons do not. Set up
your four banks in advance and the music never drops out mid-performance.

## How many effect lines there are

type: quiz
id: q-2-44
cite: guide-book 24 "Two effect lines are provided, `FX1` and `FX2`, and you can apply one or both at the same time."
tags: effects, core

How many effect lines does this unit provide, and can you use them together?

- [x] Two — `FX1` and `FX2` — and you can apply one or both at the same time
- [ ] Two, but only one can be active at a time
- [ ] Four, one per bank button
- [ ] One, shared by all pads

### Why

Two independent lines mean you can stack a tone-shaping effect on FX1 with a
rhythmic effect on FX2 — the effect chapters treat them as one instrument.

You press the `FX1` button so it lights up. Now what does turning the FX1 dial do?

- [x] It changes how strongly the effect is applied
- [ ] It selects the type of effect
- [ ] It mutes the effect line
- [ ] It switches the dial to controlling FX2

### Why

Lit button = playing the effect: one dial is both selector and intensity control, and
the button's light tells you which mode you are in.

## What the effect applies to

type: quiz
id: q-2-47
cite: guide-book 24 "the effect is applied to the entire sound that is playing"
tags: effects, core

Several pads' sounds are layered and an effect is selected. Which sounds does the
effect apply to?

### Answer

The entire sound that is playing — and also the sounds of pads you tap after selecting
the effect.

### Why

Effects sit on the whole mix, not on one pad: everything currently sounding and
everything you add next passes through them, which is what makes a filter sweep move
the entire performance at once.
