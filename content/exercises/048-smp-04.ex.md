## Change a sample between one-shot and looped playback

type: drill
id: d-3-07
cite: guide-book 31 "you can change how it sounds using the"
tags: sampling, core

Take a sample you just recorded and flip it between one-shot and looped playback,
using the `ONE SHOT` and `LOOP` buttons you know from Part: Pad play.

### Step

cite: guide-book 31 "A sample immediately after recording is treated as a one-shot sound"
check: The pad plays your sample once to the end and stops on its own, with `ONE SHOT` lit.

Tap your freshly recorded pad and watch the function button row: a sample immediately
after recording is treated as a one-shot sound.

### Step

cite: guide-book 21 "the sound plays repeatedly"
check: The same pad now repeats your sample over and over until you tap it again.

With that pad's sound selected, turn the `LOOP` button on and tap the pad.

### Step

cite: guide-book 21 "the sound plays to the end, then stops"
check: Tapping the pad plays the sample once to the end again, exactly as before.

Turn `LOOP` back off to restore the original one-shot behavior.

## Record from an external source

type: drill
id: d-3-09
cite: guide-book 28 "You can also record from an instrument or external device connected to this unit"
tags: sampling, stretch

Sample from an instrument on the AUDIO IN (♪) jack, or a smartphone on the DATA port,
instead of the built-in microphone.

### Step

cite: guide-book 8 "turn off the power of this unit and all external devices"
check: Both this unit and the external device are powered on, volumes still low, with no distortion.

Turn off this unit and the external device, connect them (instrument to the AUDIO IN
jack, or smartphone to the DATA port with the included USB cable), turn all volumes
down low, then power on this unit first and the external device second.

### Step

cite: guide-book 28 "To record an instrument performance or the like"
check: The `INPUT SELECT` switch sits at `♪` for an instrument, or `USB` for a smartphone.

Set the `INPUT SELECT` switch to match the connection: `♪` for the AUDIO IN jack,
`USB` for the DATA port.

### Step

cite: guide-book 28 "does not peak out (swing all the way) while recording"
check: The L and R meters swing with the source but never swing all the way to the end.

Enter Sampling mode, play the source, and balance the device's volume against
`INPUT VOL` so the level meter does not peak out.

### Step

cite: guide-book 30 "A pad with a sample assigned to it lights up yellow"
check: The pad lights up yellow and tapping it plays the external sound.

Sample to an empty pad exactly as you did with the microphone: select the pad, let the
sound start the recording, then stop it.

## What actually starts an AUTO TRIGGER recording

type: quiz
id: q-3-19
cite: guide-book 30 "Speak toward the microphone, and recording starts automatically"
tags: sampling, core

You are in Sampling mode with `AUTO TRIGGER` and you have already selected pad `4`.
What starts the recording?

- [x] Sound reaching the microphone
- [ ] Pressing the `REC` button a second time
- [ ] Confirming with the `A` button
- [ ] Nothing -- it started the moment you selected the pad

### Why

With `AUTO TRIGGER` the unit arms itself on pad selection and then waits: speak toward
the microphone and recording starts automatically. "It started when you selected the
pad" describes `MANUAL TRIGGER`, not this setting.

## How the unit shows a recording in progress

type: quiz
id: q-3-20
cite: guide-book 30 "the meter swings in time with the sound"
tags: sampling, intro

While a recording is in progress, how can you tell -- from the panel and from the
display?

### Answer

The `REC` button is lit (no longer flashing), and the level meter on the display swings
in time with the sound.

## Stopping a recording

type: quiz
id: q-3-21
cite: guide-book 30 "To stop recording, press pad"
tags: sampling, core

How do you stop a recording in progress?

- [x] Tap the selected pad again, or press the `REC` button
- [ ] Press the `A` button
- [ ] Press the `DEL` button
- [ ] Wait -- recording always stops by itself after four counts

### Why

Either the pad you are recording to or the `REC` button ends the take -- whichever is
handier for your timing. Nothing stops it automatically, so a late stop just records
silence you will want to trim.

## The REC button after the take

type: quiz
id: q-3-22
cite: guide-book 30 "the REC button highlighted and the callout"
tags: sampling, intro

Recording has just stopped. What does the `REC` button do?

- [x] It goes unlit
- [ ] It keeps flashing
- [ ] It stays lit
- [ ] It lights up yellow

### Why

Lit means recording, flashing means armed and waiting in Sampling mode, unlit means the
take is over -- the button's state tells you which phase you are in at a glance.

## What color marks a newly assigned pad

type: quiz
id: q-3-23
cite: guide-book 30 "A pad with a sample assigned to it lights up yellow"
tags: sampling, core

A pad that has just had a sample assigned to it lights up in which color?

- [x] Yellow
- [ ] White
- [ ] Red
- [ ] It stays unlit

### Why

Yellow is the default lighting color for a pad with a sample assigned. White is what a
pad shows while being played or while selected for deletion, and unlit means empty.

## Checking a fresh recording

type: quiz
id: q-3-24
cite: guide-book 30 "again to check the sound"
tags: sampling, intro

Right after a recording stops, how do you check what you captured?

### Answer

Tap the pad -- the sample assigned to it plays back immediately.

### Why

There is no separate preview screen: the pad itself is the playback button, so checking
a take costs one tap and you can re-record right away if you dislike it.

## The counting habit

type: quiz
id: q-3-25
cite: guide-book 30 "you can create samples that are easy to work with"
tags: sampling, core

What habit does the Guide Book recommend so that your samples come out easy to work
with?

### Answer

Before recording, count "1, 2, 3, 4" in your head, then speak and stop with good
timing -- so the sample fits neatly into counts.

### Why

A sample that fits one or two counts drops cleanly into rhythm patterns and the
sequencer later; a take with ragged ends fights the beat every time you use it.
