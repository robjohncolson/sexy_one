# Operating precautions

- This unit is made up of precision electronic components. Failure to observe the following can make it impossible to maintain data correctly and can cause malfunction.
  - Do not drop the unit, place heavy objects on it, strike it hard, or apply pressure to it. Doing so can cause malfunction. Also take care not to put it in a bag together with hard objects.
  - Do not operate the buttons or pads with a ballpoint pen or other pointed object.
  - Do not apply strong force to the display or poke it with a ballpoint pen or other pointed object.
    The screen is made of glass, so it can be scratched or cracked.
  - Do not disassemble the unit. Malfunction caused by disassembly will require charged repair even within the warranty period.
  - Do not use the unit in locations prone to static electricity.
- Do not connect the unit in any way not described in this manual.
- When connecting to the DATA port, use the included USB Type-C™ cable.
- The MAIN VOL of this unit is shared between the headphones and the speaker. Even when the slider is at the same position, the actual volume level differs between them, so before turning the power on or connecting headphones, set the unit's MAIN VOL to minimum. Be especially careful when switching from the speaker to headphones. To protect your hearing, raise the volume gradually.
- If the power is cut during recording, the data being recorded may be lost.
  For long recording sessions, use the USB AC adaptor.
  When running on batteries, check the remaining battery charge before recording.
- When no sound is playing, faint "hiss" or "buzz" noise may be heard from the speaker and headphones depending on the connection method and environment. This noise is not a product malfunction; it arises from the characteristics of electronic circuits.
- When turning the power on, a sudden popping or crackling sound may occur. If using headphones, put them on after turning the power on.
- The built-in microphone is designed for close-range sound pickup, so use it with the sound source close to the mic. For important recordings, we recommend making a test recording beforehand to check the distance to the mic, the surrounding noise, and so on.
- This unit includes a screen saver function to prevent display burn-in (this is different from the Auto Power Off function).
  If no operation is performed for about 30 minutes, the display turns off automatically. It can be restored by a button operation or pad operation.

## Operating environment

- Operating temperature range: 0°C to 40°C
- Do not place the unit in locations such as the following.
  - Extremely hot or cold locations outside the operating temperature range
  - Locations exposed to direct sunlight, high humidity or dust, or large amounts of sand
  - Near air conditioning or heating equipment, or anywhere else with extreme changes in temperature or humidity
  - Inside a car during the day, or locations subject to strong vibration
- When using or storing the unit, keep it away from devices that generate strong electromagnetic waves/radiation or magnetic fields.
- Use the unit out of the reach of small children.
- The unit is not waterproof. Do not touch it with wet hands or let it get wet.
- When cleaning, use a dry, soft cloth.
  When the unit is dirty, wipe it with a dry, soft cloth. Rubbing hard with a cloth may scratch the display. If dirt is heavy, soak a soft cloth in diluted neutral detergent, wring it out firmly, wipe the dirt off lightly, and then finish by wiping lightly with a dry, soft cloth. Do not use thinner, benzine, cosmetics, or other volatile solvents. Doing so could erase the lettering on the buttons and pads or stain the case. When disinfecting, wipe two or three times with a non-alcohol disinfecting sheet, then finish by wiping lightly with a dry, soft cloth.
- Beware of chemicals.
  If thinner, gasoline (benzine), kerosene, various solvents, oils or greases, or cleaners, alcohols (including alcohol-type disinfecting sheets), adhesives, paints, chemicals, insect repellent sprays, sunscreens, cosmetics, alkaline detergents, wax, or oily foods (instant ramen, food soups containing pork broth, etc.) and the like adhere to the resin case, they can cause discoloration, deterioration, peeling of the coating, or cracking. Please take care.
- Keep magnetic cards away.
  Do not bring credit cards, bank cards, prepaid cards, or other items carrying magnetic strips extremely close to this unit. Because this unit uses magnetized components, magnetic cards may become unusable.

- When connecting a USB cable to this unit, always connect it with the unit's power turned off.
- If a USB AC adaptor is connected to the POWER/DATA port while batteries are installed in the unit, USB power takes priority.
- When not using the unit, unplug the USB cable.
- When using the separately available USB AC adaptor, carefully read the user documentation included with the USB AC adaptor and use it correctly.
- If the USB cable comes loose, the power may cut off even when batteries are installed, and data being recorded and settings may be lost. Make sure the cable is securely connected.
- If a smartphone is connected to the POWER/DATA port, power may be supplied from the smartphone to this unit (in some cases it may not be). If you want to communicate without drawing power from the smartphone, connect the smartphone to the DATA port and the USB AC adaptor to the POWER/DATA port.

## About the batteries

- This unit can also be used with commercially available AAA rechargeable nickel-metal hydride eneloop batteries (hereinafter, "eneloop") or AAA alkaline batteries.
  Do not use any batteries other than those specified with this unit. Due to a mismatch between the battery characteristics and the unit's specifications, the batteries may not achieve their rated battery life, or malfunction may result.
  - ※ As a guideline, continuous operation on batteries is approximately 2 hours when using eneloop. When using alkaline batteries, or depending on usage conditions, this may drop to 1 hour or less.
    For extended playing or operation, we recommend supplying power with a USB AC adaptor.
- If the batteries come loose during use, information such as data being recorded and settings may be lost. Make sure the battery cover is securely attached when using the unit.
- eneloop batteries, chargers, and alkaline batteries are not included with this product. Purchase eneloop batteries, chargers, and alkaline batteries at a retailer or similar outlet.
- When using eneloop batteries, note the following.
  - Always use the dedicated charger to charge eneloop batteries.
  - eneloop batteries cannot be charged while installed in this unit.
  - When using eneloop batteries and their dedicated charger, carefully read the user documentation and precautions included with each product, and use them correctly.
- Depending on how the unit is used, battery life may become significantly shorter. In particular, setting the LED brightness high or playing through the speaker at maximum volume drains the batteries faster, so take care.
  Also, when the unit is used at low temperatures, battery capacity drops due to the characteristics of batteries, making "REPLACE THE BATTERY" more likely to appear on the display. In that case, we recommend supplying power with a USB AC adaptor.
- Depending on sound playback/recording activity and usage conditions (remaining battery charge, environment, etc.), the power may shut off without "REPLACE THE BATTERY" being displayed, or the power may shut off immediately after "REPLACE THE BATTERY" appears on the display. Take particular care while recording, because recorded data may be lost.

> **Note:** If Auto Power Off is set to off, the power will no longer shut off automatically, so the unit will continue to consume power.

## Replacing the batteries

When "REPLACE THE BATTERY" appears on the display, replace the batteries as soon as possible. Even if it does not appear, be sure to replace the batteries once a month.
In particular, leaving depleted eneloop or alkaline batteries in this unit can degrade the eneloop batteries or cause the alkaline batteries to leak, so remove them immediately.

- Continuing to use this unit with depleted batteries can cause the power to shut off suddenly. This creates the risk of corruption or loss of the data stored on the unit.
- Always turn the power off before replacing the batteries. Replacing the batteries without turning off the power can cause settings and other information to be lost.
- When replacing, always replace with four fully charged eneloop batteries or four new alkaline batteries. Using batteries other than those specified, or mixing new and old batteries, can significantly shorten battery life or cause malfunction.
- After switching to eneloop batteries, set the battery setting (BATTERY Type) to "BAT-e" (for the battery setting, see p. 55).
  If the power shuts off after "REPLACE THE BATTERY" appears on the display, you can resume use by switching the power switch "OFF→ON" once.

■ **Keep backup copies of important data.**
Back up any important content stored on this unit. Stored content may be lost due to malfunction, repair, or other causes (for the backup settings, see p. 60).

■ **Auto Power Off**
This unit is equipped with an "Auto Power Off function" that automatically turns off the power when no operation is performed for a set period of time.

- Set time
  Default setting: 20 minutes
- How to restore power
  If Auto Power Off has activated, you can resume use by switching the power switch "OFF→ON" once.
- Caution
  Note that when the Auto Power Off setting is "OFF", the unit continues to consume power (for the Auto Power Off function setting, see p. 55).

## About the SXC-1's Beat Sync feature

This feature makes multiple looped sounds play so they sound natural when layered on top of each other. By default it is OFF; turn it ON in the system settings (see PART 4, p. 55) to use it.

### Time stretch

While a sound that has a BPM setting is playing, if you play another sound with a different BPM, it is played back **matched to the tempo of the sound that is already playing**. For a sound that has a pitch, only the tempo changes — the pitch stays the same.

*[Screenshot: display showing `MASTER BPM:130`, `BANK B:2  PAD:13  BPM:130` above a waveform]*

This is the display when you tap a pad that has a BPM setting. The BPM at the upper right is the sound's tempo information, and the same value is also shown in the MASTER BPM area at the top.

*[Screenshot: display showing `MASTER BPM:130`, `BANK A:1  PAD:11  BPM:110` above a waveform]*

If you play another sound while a MASTER BPM is set, it is played at the MASTER BPM tempo, regardless of its original tempo.

*[Screenshot: display showing `MASTER BPM:110`, `BANK A:1  PAD:11  BPM:110` above a waveform]*

Stopping playback resets the MASTER BPM. If you then play the later sound again, the MASTER BPM changes to the BPM shown at the upper right, and the sound plays at that tempo.

### Quantize

When you play one looped sound and then trigger another looped sound, this automatically corrects slight timing errors in when you tap the pad, aligning the start of the looped sounds precisely for playback.

## What the ONE SHOT button and LOOP button do

*[Figure: function button row showing `▶/■`, `REC`, `ONE SHOT`, `LOOP`, `DEL`, `EDIT`, with `ONE SHOT` and `LOOP` highlighted]*

### ONE SHOT button

| State | Behavior |
|---|---|
| When ON *(button lit)* | Tap the pad and the sound plays to the end. Tap again before it finishes, and playback starts over from the beginning. |
| When OFF *(button unlit)* | The sound plays only while you hold the pad down and stops when you release your finger. Tap again and playback starts over from the beginning. |

### LOOP button

| State | Behavior |
|---|---|
| When ON *(button lit)* | Tap the pad and the sound plays repeatedly. It stops when you tap the pad again or press the play/stop button. |
| When OFF *(button unlit)* | Tap the pad and the sound plays to the end, then stops. |

These buttons set whether a sound plays once ("one-shot sound") or plays repeatedly ("looped sound"). The setting is remembered as is for each pad.
The preset sounds come preconfigured with settings that suit their content.

### With each button state, sounds play as follows.

*[Figure: four rows, each showing the ONE SHOT and LOOP button states, the resulting playback waveforms, and a bar labeled "time the pad is held down"]*
- Row 1: ONE SHOT lit, LOOP unlit — each tap plays the sound to the end; label: "time the pad is held down"
- Row 2: ONE SHOT unlit, LOOP unlit — sound plays only while the pad is held; label: "time the pad is held down"
- Row 3: ONE SHOT lit, LOOP lit — a tap starts repeated playback until stopped; label: "time the pad is held down"
- Row 4: ONE SHOT unlit, LOOP lit — the sound repeats while the pad is held; label: "time the pad is held down"

Using the ONE SHOT button and LOOP button skillfully lets you play in more advanced ways — for example, cutting off the tail of a long sound partway through for a percussive effect, or looping a short sound.

## Effects explained

The effects provided for `FX1` and `FX2`, and what each dial changes, are as follows.

*[Figure: close-up of the top panel showing the two dials on either side of the INPUT VOL and MAIN VOL sliders, with the left dial circled and labeled FX1 and the right dial circled and labeled FX2]*

### FX1

#### FILTER (filter)

An effect that muffles or lightens the sound. The cutoff frequency, resonance, and filter type change in combination.

#### FLANGER (flanger)

An effect that adds a metallic swirl to the sound. The depth, rate, feedback, and mix change in combination.

#### PHASER (phaser)

This effect also adds a swirl to the sound, but it changes the sound in a different way than the flanger. The number of stages, rate, depth, feedback, and mix change in combination.

#### BIT CRUSHER (bit crusher)

Adds digital noise to the sound and distorts it. The bit depth, sample rate, and dry/wet change in combination.

### FX2

#### ROLL 1 (roll 1)

Produces an effect as if a pad were being tapped in a rapid series of finely spaced hits.

#### ROLL 1/2 (roll 1/2)

Produces an effect as if a pad were being tapped repeatedly at even finer spacing.

#### ROLL 1/4 (roll 1/4)

Produces an effect as if a pad were being tapped repeatedly at still finer spacing.

#### ROLL PATTERN (roll pattern)

Produces an effect as if a pad were being tapped repeatedly in a certain fixed pattern.

#### DELAY 3/4 (delay 3/4)

Adds a delayed copy of the original sound, producing an echo-like effect.

#### DELAY 3/16 (delay 3/16)

Adds a slightly delayed copy of the original sound, producing an echo-like effect.

FX2 varies continuously from dry (effect 0) to wet (effect 100).

## Outdo even pro drummers with just your fingers!?

The great appeal of the style of playing sounds by tapping pads is that you can enjoy performing music even if you can't play an instrument. Finger drumming in particular is a popular field, with some masters pulling off jaw-dropping plays that surpass what a drummer can do. ## The trick is to decide which finger taps each pad

Finger drumming, which involves fine movements of all ten fingers, may feel difficult until you get used to it. First, fix which finger taps what — for example, kick = thumb, snare = index finger, hi-hat = middle finger — and start from a slow tempo. Practicing in separate combinations, such as kick and snare only, or hi-hat and snare only, is also recommended.

### Sampling changed the style of music production

Sampling — the technique of making music using existing sounds as material — was born in the 1980s. At first, only very short sounds could be sampled, but once longer phrases and rhythm patterns could also be handled, it came to be used heavily in hip-hop, house, and other genres, and the music production process changed dramatically. That technology is also used in the sounds of electronic musical instruments.

## ■ AUTO Trig Lv

When AUTO TRIGGER is selected in Sampling mode, sets the volume at which recording starts automatically, in 5 levels from 1 to 5 (-28 dB to -12 dB in 4 dB steps / default is 3). The smaller the value, the quieter the sound at which recording starts. Set it so that recording doesn't start on an unintended sound — or, conversely, so that recording doesn't fail to start.

## ■ Beat Sync

Sets the automatic beat matching described on p. 18 to ON/OFF. The default is OFF.

## ■ LED Bright

Sets the brightness of the pad and button LEDs in 5 levels from 0 to 4 (0 = unlit). The default is 3.

## ■ Disp Bright

Sets the brightness of the display in 5 levels from 1 to 5. The default is 5.

## ■ APO Time

Sets the time until Auto Power Off: 20 minutes, 1 hour, 2 hours, or off. The default is 20 minutes.

## ■ BATTERY Type

Switches between BAT-A (alkaline batteries) and BAT-e (eneloop). The default is BAT-A.

## ■ POWER Supply

When running on USB power, a power-cord icon is displayed. When running on batteries, the remaining charge is displayed in 3 levels.

## ■ FW Version

Displays the firmware version (view only).

## ■ SERIAL No.

# Troubleshooting

*Appendix*

## If these symptoms occur

### The power does not turn on

- Check that the USB AC adaptor is connected properly and that the batteries are inserted in the correct orientation (see p. 12).
- Replace the batteries with new ones (sold separately) (see p. 9).
- If you are using eneloop batteries, recharge them.

### The power turns off even though nothing is being operated

- The power was turned off by the Auto Power Off function.
  Turn the power back on.
- The batteries are running low.
  Replace the batteries with new ones (sold separately) (see p. 9).
- If you are using eneloop batteries, recharge them.
- The display is turned off by the screen saver function.
  You can restore it by operating a button or a pad.

### The Auto Power Off function does not work

- Check the Auto Power Off setting (see p. 55).
- The Auto Power Off function does not operate during sound playback.

### Battery life is shorter than stated

- The LED brightness is set high.
  Lower the brightness setting (see p. 55).

### The display suddenly goes off, or the unit restarts

- The batteries are running low.
  Replace the batteries with new ones (sold separately) (see p. 9).
- If you are using eneloop batteries, recharge them.

<!-- page 64 -->

*Troubleshooting — Appendix*

### Cannot communicate

- The connection is not correct.
  Check that this unit and the smartphone are correctly connected with the USB cable (see p. 56).
- Use the included USB Type-C cable (see p. 12).

### No sound can be heard

- The volume is not set appropriately.
  Adjust the volume (see p. 10).
- When headphones are connected, no sound comes from the speaker.
  Unplug the headphones.
- The headphones are not connected correctly.
  Check that the headphone plug is fully inserted all the way in.
- You are using incompatible headphones.
  Use headphones with a 3.5 mm stereo mini (3-pole) plug.

### The sound is distorted

- The volume is too high.
  Adjust the volume (see p. 10).
- The batteries are running low.
  Replace the batteries with new ones (see p. 9).

### The display does not light, or it flashes

- The batteries are running low.
  Replace the batteries with new ones (see p. 9).

### The display is off, but the pads are lit

- To protect the screen, the display turns off after 30 minutes (screen saver function).
  You can restore it by operating a button or a pad.

<!-- page 65 -->

*Troubleshooting — Appendix*

### No sound is recorded

- The recording input level is not appropriate.
  Adjust `INPUT VOL` or the volume of the external device (see p. 28).
- Check that the input source used for recording (side-panel `INPUT SELECT` switch) matches the corresponding jack (`USB` or `AUDIO IN`) (see p. 28).
- Check that the plug is inserted correctly, all the way in (see p. 13).

## Try making some sound

### Performing with the preset sounds

Preset sounds are assigned to this unit in advance. Press the **A button** to select bank 1. If bank 1 is not selected on the **A button**, you can change it with the **directional buttons**.

*[Figure: sequence of images — pressing the A button among the A/B/C/D buttons → `SELECT BANK 1` screen → pressing a pad on the 4×4 pad grid → Performance mode screen (`MASTER BPM:95` / `BANK A:1  PAD:10  BPM:95` with waveform)]*

In the preset sounds, sound data suited to looped sounds is assigned to the orange and purple pads, and sound data suited to one-shot sounds is assigned to the yellow and blue pads.

> **Looped sound:**
> Sound data created on the premise of repeated playback (looping) — mainly rhythm and melody patterns that form the background of a song.
>
> **One-shot sound:**
> Single-hit sound data meant to be played only once, such as drum kicks, snares, and sound effects.

First, press an orange or purple pad to play a looped sound.

Along with the sound being played, freely press the yellow and blue pads and try performing.

*[Figure: hand pressing a pad on the 4×4 pad grid]*

### Changing how sounds play

Try changing how sounds play with the **ONE SHOT/LOOP buttons**. By turning both **ONE SHOT** and **LOOP** ON, a pad pressed once will keep playing back repeatedly.

*[Figure: row of function buttons ▶/■, REC, ONE SHOT, LOOP, DEL, EDIT, with a hand pressing ONE SHOT and LOOP]*

| Button | When the setting is ON | When the setting is OFF |
|---|---|---|
| ONE SHOT | Pressing the pad plays the sound to the end | The sound plays only while the pad is held down |
| LOOP | When the sound plays to the end, the playback position automatically returns to the beginning and playback repeats | When the sound plays to the end, playback stops there |

You can stop a sound that is playing by pressing the **▶/■ button**.

## Troubleshooting

If a problem occurs, check and address the following.

### No sound

- Adjust the volume with the **MAIN VOL** slider.
- Check that headphones, external speakers, or the like are correctly connected to the **PHONE** or **LINE OUT jack**.

> **Note:** When headphones or the like are connected to the **PHONE jack**, no sound comes from the built-in speaker.

### Cannot record

- Check that the **INPUT SELECT switch** is in the correct position.
- The remaining storage space may be insufficient. Use the **DEL button** to delete sounds you do not need.
- Select an empty pad and start recording.
- Check that the external device is connected.

### Cannot connect with the app

- Check that this unit and the smartphone are connected with the included USB Type-C cable.

### Power does not turn on

- Check that the USB AC adaptor is connected to this unit's **POWER/DATA port**.
- Replace the batteries with new ones.

### When you want to initialize (return to the factory default state)

When you want to initialize the settings, you can do so with the following procedure. Also, if you have deleted preset sounds, initialization can restore that data.

> **Note:** Be aware that initializing deletes any data you have sampled.

1. Long-press the **EDIT button**.
   The system settings appear.
2. Use the **directional buttons** (up/down) to select [Initialize], then press the **directional button** (right).
   A confirmation screen appears.
3. Select the **A button**.
   Initialization of this unit starts.
